# Community outreach

## Who
- **Independent career coaches** (search LinkedIn for "career coach" + "job search"; many post weekly)
- **University career centers** (the "employer relations" or "career services" contact)
- **Laid-off and job-seeker communities**: Discord servers, Slack groups, Meetups, local workforce boards
- **Newsletter writers** who cover job hunting or layoffs

## How
- One person at a time, written for them. No mass emails.
- Offer something, don't ask for something: free Pro for their clients for a semester, or a 15-minute walkthrough.
- If they say no or don't reply, leave it there.

## Template: career coach (edit it, don't paste it as-is)

> Subject: A free tool for your clients' job searches
>
> Hi [name], I read your post about [specific thing they wrote]. Agreed, especially the part about [detail].
>
> I built a free tool called JobSignal. You paste a job link and it checks whether the listing is live on the company's own job board, how long it's really been up, and whether the company has filed layoff notices or SEC restructurings. It shows sources for everything and doesn't make claims it can't back up.
>
> Would it be useful for your clients? If you'd like, I can set you up with Pro for free so you can watch listings for them. And honestly, I'd value your take on what it gets wrong.
>
> [your name]

## Template: university career center

> Subject: Free job-posting checker for students
>
> Hello, I'm [name]. I built JobSignal, a free tool that helps job seekers check listings before applying: whether the posting is live on the employer's own job board, when it was first published, scam-pattern checks (chat-app interviews, requests for bank details), and public layoff records for the employer.
>
> Students are a common target for job scams, and I thought it might be useful alongside the guidance you already give. It's free, needs no account for basic checks, and doesn't collect resumes or personal data.
>
> Happy to send a short walkthrough or answer questions.
